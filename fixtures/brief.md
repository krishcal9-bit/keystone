## Summary
Oakfield Community Church (sample) in Santa Ana appears to qualify for SB 4 by-right approval: a congregation that has owned the parcel since 1979, with the law in effect today. Keeping 60 of its 120 parking spaces leaves about 2.45 buildable acres, enough for roughly 107 Habitat homes as stacked flats (364 people, 4,815 affordability-years). The site scores 100/100 on fit: close to transit, schools and a grocery store, flat, with no mapped hazards.

## Top 3 risks
- Density: two-story townhomes alone would fall short of the 40-per-acre minimum that applies in this institutional zone, so part of the site needs stacked flats.
- Parking: Sunday peak demand must be counted before committing to 60 kept spaces.
- Consensus: a land partnership needs a board vote and clear terms on control, reversion and who manages the homes.

## Next 5 steps
1. Order a Phase I environmental site assessment (Phase II only if the Phase I warrants it).
2. Run a title check confirming ownership on or before January 1, 2024.
3. Ask the board for a vote to explore a partnership, with no commitment yet.
4. Book a pre-application meeting with Santa Ana planning on SB 4 objective standards.
5. Schedule a Habitat OC site visit and a peak-day parking count.

## Questions for the congregation
- How many spaces do you use on your busiest day of the year?
- Which parts of the campus must stay exactly as they are?
- Are there families in your community who would want to apply for a Habitat home here?
- Who on the board should lead this conversation?
